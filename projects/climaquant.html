ClimaQuant

A climate-adjusted stock options pricing platform bridging natural and economic variability. This project was developed in collaboraion with two other students at Georgia Tech, Abhinav Sharma and Emily Li, as part of our submission for the Hacklytics Hackathon 2026.

Overview

The goal of this project is to create an innovative way of interacting with the financial world. We do this through a creative solution that integrates climate data into stock option trading strategies within a website anyone can use as a tool. By uniting climate data with financial data, we seek to break the barriers separating natural variability from economical variability. After all, as digital technology and AI further entrench data from human systems, it is imperative we always account for the natural systems upon which these human systems rely.

What are Stock Options?

Imagine you're a trader hoping to make significant money. Stock options let you buy a stock for a fixed price in the future — protecting you from risk while preserving upside. For example, you negotiate with a company to buy a $100 stock at a set strike price (K) in one year. To compensate for potential gains they'd miss out on, they charge you an upfront option price (C), calculated using the Black-Scholes equation:

What We Built

We developed an application that allows users to calculate stock option pricing under climate-based and nonclimate-based scenarios. Users can vary strike price, maturation period, and the risk-free rate (r) to visualize different scenarios across major companies spanning energy, food and agriculture, and retail. We implemented Black-Scholes option-pricing using company stock data with climate-enrichment, before then coupling volatility with climate variables (and implementing autoregression) and interpreting and comparing the outputs of each. SphinxAI was a crucial component of our backend in terms of creating sophisticated, insightful visualizations of key economic trends. For example, absolute average percent error for option prices was one metric analyzed. This parameter measures the average change between the stock price at the time of maturation and the strike price plus the calculated accumulation of the option price. The closer this is to zero, the more “accurate” the option price “prediction”.

Data & Technology

Backend data was sourced from Stooq (stock market) and NOAA (heating/cooling degree days, Palmer Z Drought Index). We implemented Black-Scholes pricing with climate-enriched volatility, autoregression, and SphinxAI for sophisticated trend visualizations. ClaudeAI was instrumental in frontend formatting, styling, and backend integration.
